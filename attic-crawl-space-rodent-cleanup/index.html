Why this job comes last.

Cleanup seems like the natural opener, yet it pays off only once nothing lives inside and the holes are plugged. Otherwise the new insulation becomes the next nest. The sequence runs survey, traps, seals and finally cleanup.

It is also the step most affected by health guidance, which is why the CDC link below matters. Dried droppings and nest material can release dust that is unsafe to breathe, so the method keeps everything damp.

What the crew does, in order.

  • Airs out the space and sets up protective gear.
  • Soaks droppings and nests in disinfectant before anyone handles them.
  • Removes soiled insulation and debris into heavy bags.
  • Wipes framing, ducts and the floor of the space.
  • Lets surfaces dry and checks for stains, chewed wires or damp spots.
  • Reports what was removed and whether new insulation is needed.

Typical protective gear is gloves, goggles and a respirator. The point is to prevent dust and contact, not to rush.

Heat, humidity and attic work.

Attics in warm, humid climates can be very hot. We schedule the work for the part of the day when it is safest and take breaks as needed. Mention any past leak, since damp insulation behaves differently from dry and may call for other steps.

What you do before the crew arrives.

Clear the path to the hatch and make sure the lights work. Let us know about stored items in the attic, and move anything valuable or sentimental out beforehand. Keep children and pets away from the hatch area.

After the cleanout.

The space is aired and checked. If everything is sealed and clean, you can decide on replacement insulation. If anything shows new gnawing, the technician traces it before the job closes.

Waiting for the appointment.

  • Latch every bin lid tonight and roll the bins away from the garage wall.
  • If cleaning cannot wait, soak the spot with disinfectant before wiping with paper towels.
  • Raise the pet dishes onto a shelf overnight.
  • Crouch beside the garage door and note any strip of light along its base.

Questions about attic cleanup.

Could I clear the attic on my own?

A small amount can be handled by following the CDC steps below. Big nests, heavy droppings or a cramped crawl space need protective gear and a crew.

Do I have to replace all the insulation?

Not always. Soiled sections are removed, and clean sections stay. The technician marks what is contaminated and what is not.

Why delay cleanup until the animals are out?

Fresh insulation gets fouled again if animals can still get in, so trapping and sealing go ahead of it.

Will the smell go away?

Hauling out droppings and nests removes most of the smell, and airing plus surface treatment handles the rest. A carcass inside a wall is its own task.

Is the work disruptive to my family?

The crew works in the attic or crawl space and uses the shortest path to the door. You may be asked to keep pets and children away from that route.

Getting ready for a cleanout.

Tell us how to reach the space, the clearance overhead and any insulation that seems pressed down, discolored or pushed to one side. Cleaning only begins after the animals are out.
