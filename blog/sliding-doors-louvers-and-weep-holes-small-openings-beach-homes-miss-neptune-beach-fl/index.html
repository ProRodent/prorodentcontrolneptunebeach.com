Sliding Doors, Louvers and Weep Holes: Small Openings Beach Homes Miss

Most homeowners check the big gaps and miss the small ones. Here are the track, louver and drain openings that a mouse can use, and how to look at each.

A house can have a sound roof and tight doors and still leave several small openings that a mouse treats as a front gate. These are the gaps people stop noticing: the track under a sliding door, the louver on a laundry room wall, the weep hole near a window sill.

This article looks at those places. It is general advice for homes near the coast, not a statement on how any specific building stands. Salt air and humidity can speed up wear on seals and metal, so it makes sense to look at these parts more often, whatever your neighborhood.

Sliding glass doors.

The bottom track of a sliding door often collects dirt and leaves. The seal at the corners and along the edge wears down. Even a quarter-inch slot lets a mouse pass.

  • Look along the bottom edge and corners with the door closed.
  • Check the rubber or brush seals for flattening or tears.
  • Clean the track so you can see what is underneath.
  • Replace worn brushes with new ones, and add a metal threshold guard if gaps remain.

Not every opening should be sealed. Vents and weep holes do a job. The aim is to keep the function and block the animal.

Pipe and cable entries.

Where a pipe, a cable or a hose bib passes through an exterior wall, there is often a ring of gap around it. This ring can be an inch deep and wide enough for a mouse. It often goes unnoticed because plantings or fixtures cover it.

  • Look behind shrubs and under fixtures.
  • Check the gap around gas and water lines.
  • Press on the sealant to see whether it has cracked or crumbled.
  • Use a steel collar or packed mesh with sealant to close the ring.

Garage and storage doors.

A garage door that meets the slab unevenly leaves a wedge-shaped gap. Corners are weak, since the rubber seal pulls away from the track. See the garage page for the whole-room view, and test the door in the evening by looking for light from inside.

Why small openings matter.

An animal that finds an opening tends to return to it. Others may follow the scent trail. A small gap that goes unsealed can become the main route, and the damage grows over time as teeth enlarge it.

Closing the gap early costs little, usually a collar or a strip of mesh. Waiting costs more, since a colony inside means trapping, cleanup and repairs on top of the sealing.

A Saturday morning plan.

  • Walk the outside of the house and photograph each vent, louver and weep hole.
  • Test each door with a flashlight held at the bottom edge.
  • Check each pipe and cable entry for a ring of gap.
  • Note anything corroded, bent or missing.
  • Call if you see droppings, gnawing or a hole larger than a finger.

You do not need to fix everything on the day. A list with photos is useful to a technician and to any contractor who helps.

What a professional adds.

A technician sees patterns across many houses and knows the weak points in common building types. He or she can also measure gaps, choose materials and test the repair. The survey page shows what the report includes.

Federal cleanup steps, boiled down.

Dust from dried waste may carry hantavirus and other germs, so the routine below steers clear of anything that kicks it into the air.

  • Reach for the spray bottle before the dustpan; nothing is touched until it is thoroughly damp.
  • Air trapped in attics, garages and sheds needs a cross-breeze before anyone enters.
  • Gloves go on at the start of the job and come off at the very end, directly into the trash bag.
  • The liquid needs roughly five minutes on the surface, because the standing time is what disinfects.
  • A second bag keeps everything sealed in until collection.
  • For this job, soap and running water beat sanitizer gel.

Still wondering about something?

What is a weep hole?

It is a small opening that lets water drain from a wall, window frame or brick course. Some are meant to stay open, so a guard or mesh insert is better than sealing them shut.

Can I fill a gap with expanding foam?

Foam can hide a gap and is easily chewed. Metal mesh or a steel collar with sealant works better.

How do I check a sliding door seal?

Close the door in a dim room and look for light along the bottom track and the corners. A flashlight from outside helps.

Are louvered vents a problem?

They allow airflow, and without a metal screen behind them they allow animals as well. Check that each has an intact screen.

Does salty air make this worse?

Salt can corrode thin metal and wear rubber over time. We have no local measurements, so we simply check these parts.
