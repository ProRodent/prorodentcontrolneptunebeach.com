Why a kitchen draws them.

Mice look for three things in one spot: shelter that stays dry, food within a short walk and a concealed corridor between them. The hollow behind a base cabinet checks every box. Garage corners with cartons leaning on the drywall do too, since the cardboard doubles as nesting stock.

Florida weather may nudge their behavior. Long wet spells could push animals toward dry framing, and sprinkler-soaked beds might do the same, but we cannot predict the month a problem will appear. A house shut up during a vacation can sit with a colony for weeks, because mice get active once the occupants are asleep.

Try a quiet listening round: park yourself by the pantry, move to the utility closet, finish at the garage wall backing the kitchen, and note any sound with its time.

Tracing signs to their source.

The survey opens on the newest evidence and radiates from it. A hand sketch notes each cluster of droppings, grease smear and torn package, and those marks typically point to a single gap. Scattering devices blindly might snag a few animals while the entrance stays wide open.

  • Beneath sinks, then the spaces beside the stove and fridge, ending at the laundry corner or air handler closet.
  • Garage side: the lower door edge, its rubber seal and the joint at the slab.
  • Up high: the attic hatch, the roof vents and each opening around a pipe or cable.
  • At grade: crawl space vents and skirting, spending extra minutes on the damper side.

Snap traps or tamper-resistant stations.

Either device type, snap trap or tamper-resistant station, runs along the wall lines mice prefer, set where kids and animals cannot reach. Mice dislike open floor and trust edges, so a single device snug against a baseboard beats a handful mid-room.

Sealing begins when first catches expose which entries are active. Mice can tug a wad of steel wool loose, so holes receive packed mesh finished with flashing or sealant. See the sealing page for materials and the trapping page for device types.

What to do before the visit.

Skip the broom for now, because sweeping lifts particles into the air. Further down, the CDC box describes damp wiping for later. Loose bait scattered indoors is a poor idea.

Food storage counts as well. Put cereal, flour and kibble in lidded tubs, and pick up the pet water bowl at bedtime. Mice already indoors will stay, but the easy buffet that lures the next batch disappears.

Seaside wear and tear.

Salt air can eat thin metal screens and wear down the brush seals on sliding doors sooner than inland conditions would. We hold no local measurements, so those parts simply get checked. Mention any sliding door, louvered vent or window unit with a worn-looking seal.

Judging whether it worked.

One quiet night is a good sign but not proof. Success is measured by empty devices across the agreed recheck window and by seals that stay firm when pressed. If scratching returns, the technician hunts for the missed opening rather than piling on more devices.

Waiting for the appointment.

  • Close off whichever room has the heaviest droppings and tell everyone at home the reason.
  • Leave the broom in the closet, since bristles stir up settled grit.
  • Move any open bait box out of toddler and pet range.
  • Write down the clock time of each thump or scratch.

What the public health pages advise.

None of this is medical advice. Think of it as a brief list of habits that public health pages recommend when rodents have been in a home.

  • Dried pellets and nests crumble into dust, so keep the broom and shop vacuum away from them.
  • Where a room has been shut, prop the doors and windows open and wait half an hour outside.
  • Slip on disposable gloves, rubber or vinyl, ahead of touching soiled material, and rinse your hands after.
  • Make a fresh bleach mix for each session, since a stale batch loses strength.
  • Tie off the bag tightly and put it in a lidded outdoor bin the same day.
  • Work the lather under your nails and up the wrists, then dry on a clean towel.

What callers ask about mice.

What size opening can a mouse use?

About the thickness of a pencil, and less if the edge flexes. Look near drain lines, behind the dryer, along door frames and where cable lines pierce stucco or siding.

Will mice leave once summer arrives?

Rarely. A shaded closet or cool garage can appeal to them in the heat, and a gap overlooked in spring often turns into the autumn entrance.

Is store-bought bait worth using while I wait?

Call first. Mice that eat bait often die within the wall, and the odor afterward can be worse than the problem you started with.

How soon will the sounds stop?

Pace is set by headcount and by how many separate entrances exist. An honest timeline comes only after the walk-through; any number given before is a guess.

Can I phone in the middle of the night?

Yes, go ahead. A live voice is on the line all night, and the earliest visit slot gets held for you.
