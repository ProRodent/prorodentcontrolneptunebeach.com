Why the survey starts outside.

A rat in an attic usually did not begin there. It reached the roof along something: a limb, a wire, a fence top or the crown of a palm. If the technician only looks inside, the route stays open and the next rat follows the same path.

Roof rats are a recognized pest in warm coastal states, and palms, dense vines and fruiting trees are the sort of cover they use. We make no claim about how many live near Neptune Beach. The walk simply asks whether anything between the ground and your eaves could serve as a ladder.

Clues that point to rats.

  • Droppings thick and blunt-ended, found in clusters along beams or in the corners of the attic.
  • Grease smudges along the tops of fences, on rafters or around a gap in the eave.
  • Gnawed fruit still hanging in a tree or dropped beneath it.
  • Steps or dragging sounds in the ceiling shortly after dusk.
  • Chewed wiring, plastic pipe or insulation near the attic hatch.

One clue is a hint. Three clues in the same area are a pattern, and the technician follows that pattern to the entrance.

How traps and sealing fit together.

Rats avoid new objects for a few days, so devices may sit baited but untouched at first. The technician places them where evidence is thickest, secures them against pets and checks them on schedule. Meanwhile the outside work proceeds: trimming advice, burrow checks and a list of roofline gaps.

Steps to take ahead of our arrival.

Bring pet food in at night and lift fallen fruit from the grass. Stack firewood or lumber away from the walls and clear vines off the siding where you can reach safely. Stay off the roof unless you are comfortable on it, because the technician will look at it anyway.

Why loose bait is a poor shortcut.

A rat that eats poison commonly crawls into a wall or the attic to die, and the smell may linger for weeks. Bait can also reach birds, pets and other wildlife. The CDC and EPA pages linked below explain the cleanup precautions and the product limits, and our approach follows them.

After the first catch.

A catch does not end the job. It tells us which route was active, and the next trip checks whether the seals on that route held. If a rat is caught but signs continue, there may be a second route we missed, and we keep searching.

Interim chores.

  • Latch every bin lid tonight and roll the bins away from the garage wall.
  • If cleaning cannot wait, soak the spot with disinfectant before wiping with paper towels.
  • Raise the pet dishes onto a shelf overnight.
  • Crouch beside the garage door and note any strip of light along its base.

A damp-wipe routine for droppings and nests.

Sequence counts: ventilate, soak, wipe, bag, wash. Every line here lines up with the federal pages listed after this box.

  • A single dry dropping can crumble and release a cloud nobody notices.
  • Where a room has been shut, prop the doors and windows open and wait half an hour outside.
  • Gloves go on at the start of the job and come off at the very end, directly into the trash bag.
  • Make a fresh bleach mix for each session, since a stale batch loses strength.
  • Tie off the bag tightly and put it in a lidded outdoor bin the same day.

What callers ask about rats.

Are the rats in my attic roof rats or Norway rats?

Roof rats favor high ground and attics, and Norway rats favor burrows and lower levels. Droppings and rub marks give hints, though the species is only settled by looking on site.

Do palms really matter?

They can. Rats may use the crowns, trunks and fallen fronds as shelter and as a bridge to the roof. We cannot count rats in any one palm, so the survey simply checks whether a crown touches your house.

How soon after trapping do the noises stop?

Often within days if the colony is small, though no timeline is certain. The return visit counts the catches and tests the seals.

Can I just trim the trees myself?

Trimming helps, and it is safest done from the ground or by a tree professional. A technician can point out which branch to cut first.

Will you use poison?

Our plans center on traps and sealing. Loose bait can lead to dead rats inside walls and puts pets and wildlife at risk, and the EPA link below explains how bait is restricted.

What helps us on a rat call.

Mention palms, dense vines, wires or branches that reach the shingles, plus any pet food kept outdoors. From those clues the technician picks the side of the house to read first.
